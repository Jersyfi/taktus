# Blueprint `software-operations`

The operation of a small software business, run by Taktus and introduced into it in six stages.
Its requirement is use case UC-03, [`docs/usecases/AF-03-software-operations.md`](../../docs/usecases/AF-03-software-operations.md):
96 processes in eleven areas, their target levels, what stays with a person, and the stages.

It is the blueprint of Taktus's second tenant (DEC-0199). The blueprint is product: its
processes name capabilities only. The tenant's instantiation — the systems behind each
capability, thresholds, anchors, roles — is the tenant's configuration on its own instance and
never enters this repository (ADR-0092, NEED-0027 §5).

Development at the tenant runs on [`dev-orchestration`](../dev-orchestration/README.md).

## State

The product provides each stage's processes; the business introduces them on its own instance
and opens each stage itself (DEC-0201). Nothing here says that a stage ran at the tenant.

[`blueprint.yaml`](blueprint.yaml) names every process with its bundle, every capability the
bundles call, the acts of UC-03 §6 that stay with a person, and the value criteria: the
blueprint an organisation instantiates with `taktusctl domain start` (ADR-0263). B-8.04 has
three bundles of one process — a cluster's, a store's and a serverless platform's — and B-8.05
and B-8.06 two each; an instance keeps one of each.

Every process is a bundle under [`processes/`](processes/), named `b-<area>-<number>`, the
identifier [`stages.yaml`](stages.yaml) names it by (ADR-0191 §4). A process that only reads and
judges starts at level 1, one that acts at level 2 (UC-03 §4). Each is registered as shipped and
run against fakes of the systems behind its capabilities in
`tests/composition/test_software_operations.py`; the cost watchers in
`tests/composition/test_cost_watcher.py`; the repository and release processes in
`tests/composition/test_software_operations_repository.py`; the logs, secrets, signing credentials, repository infrastructure and model catalogue of stage 1 in `tests/composition/test_software_operations_ops.py`; the stores, the targets beyond a cluster,
the release texts and the figures in `tests/composition/test_software_operations_release.py`, and on the providers added since — a mail service's API, an event analytics service, a headless content store, a functions platform — in `tests/composition/test_software_operations_connectors2.py`; the customer contact and back-office processes in the files of each capability's group, `tests/composition/test_software_operations_{accounts,backoffice,community,helpdesk,payments,stores,tasks}.py`. A selection over a reading is the rule `filter`,
`exact` (ADR-0192 §1); a match of two readings the rule `match`, `exact` (ADR-0199).

The product's entitlements — what a customer may use — are held in the product, and no open
standard describes them. [`connectors/entitlements.manifest.json`](connectors/entitlements.manifest.json)
is the manifest under which a tenant serves its product's own MCP server with the vendor
connector: the capabilities `entitlements.grants` and the scoped `entitlements.customers`, each
entitlement `{id, customer, product, quantity}`, the customer by the payment provider's
identifier (ADR-0199 §2). A tenant changes `tool` to its own tools' names. [`connectors/accounts.manifest.json`](connectors/accounts.manifest.json) is the second, over the product's accounts: `accounts.customers`, a customer's account and whether an address is its verified one, and `accounts.requests`, a data protection request prepared and never carried out (ADR-0208). Where the product keeps its entitlements and accounts — its own database, a document store, a backend service's collections — is the tenant's: its MCP server reads that store and answers the manifest's tools in the shapes above, and the repository builds no connector to a store whose schema only the tenant knows. A product that keeps its team chat, its support inbox, its customer system or its feedback portal itself serves each through the vendor connector under [`connectors/chat.manifest.json`](connectors/chat.manifest.json), [`connectors/helpdesk.manifest.json`](connectors/helpdesk.manifest.json), [`connectors/crm.manifest.json`](connectors/crm.manifest.json) or [`connectors/feedback.manifest.json`](connectors/feedback.manifest.json): `chat.threads`, `helpdesk.tickets`, `crm.contacts` (scoped by address) and `feedback.posts` under the operation names and shapes of the reference connectors, so every process reads them unchanged; each write takes the key as `request_id`, which the product's tool recognises itself (issue #447).

Some rows of UC-03 are parts of another process (NTC-0263): B-3.12, B-4.01, B-4.02, B-4.04
and B-4.06 are declared in B-3.02, and B-4.03 is UC-7.5's count of B-3.02's corrections. B-3.03 is B-3.02's level per
class of request (ADR-0215), and B-4.05 its scoring in dimensions (ADR-0217); B-2.05 is the model trial of UC-8.14
(NTC-0266). Stage 2
names `b-3-02` for them, so that it opens as shipped (NTC-0281); B-4.07, not built, stands in no
stage. B-2.05 and B-4.03 are served by the instance for every tenant, as the daily report B-9.01 is, and stand in no stage (NTC-0280).

[`stages.yaml`](stages.yaml) proposes UC-03's six stages, each with the processes it adds, the
level each may reach once the stage is open, and the condition under which it holds (ADR-0137,
NTC-0232). A stage opens, and holds, only while every process it adds has an active version, and
stage 0 counts the days without the daily report (ADR-0191). A tenant declares its plan from
them with `taktusctl stage declare --blueprint blueprints/software-operations/stages.yaml
--responsible <role>`, and may make every condition stricter, never looser. Its responsible role
reads the stages and opens them at `GET /stages` and `POST /stages/{stage}/open`, or by
`taktusctl stage show` and `open`.
