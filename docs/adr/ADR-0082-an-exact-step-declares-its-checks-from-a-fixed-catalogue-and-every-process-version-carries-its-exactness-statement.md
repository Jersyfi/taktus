# ADR-0082 — An exact step declares its checks from a fixed catalogue, and every process version carries its exactness statement

**Status:** accepted · builds the first part of UC-4.13 and UC-6.9 (issue #91, roadmap `0.5.0`)
on ADR-0014 and ADR-0018 · one row of the catalogue provisional under DEC-0173

## Context
ADR-0014 says that a step classed `exact` is "provably correct and machine-checkable", and that
"`exact` requires a machine check, and somebody has to write it". Until now nothing in a process
version said which check. The class was a label: a step could be `exact` with no check at all,
and nothing told a reader what was checked against what.

UC-4.13 asks for a fixed catalogue of five checks — reconciliation against a total, agreement with
a second system, plausibility bounds, approval above a threshold, sampling — each with what it
covers, what it does not cover and what it needs, and for a refusal of an `exact` step without
one. UC-6.9 asks for an exactness statement per process version, in four parts: which checks
apply, what they cover, what they do not cover, and the residual risk. It must be generated,
never edited by hand, never say "guaranteed", and never be written by a language model.

Four questions were open: where the catalogue lives, where a check is declared, where the refusal
happens, and how the statement is made.

## Decision

### 1. The catalogue is a schema of the shared kernel
`contracts/shared/v1/Check.json` is the catalogue's vocabulary. A check names its `kind` — one
row — and exactly the parameters its row needs: a reconciliation names the `total` and the
`source` it is read through; an agreement names the `source` of the second system and which value
is the `reference`; bounds name a `minimum`, a `maximum` or a `pattern`; an approval names a
`threshold` and the `role` that confirms; a sampling names a `share` and the `role` that checks.
A parameter of another row is refused. A role is a role, never a named person (principle 14).
An optional `subject` names what the check applies to in the reader's words.

A sixth row, `recomputation`, is provisional under DEC-0173: the result is what the step's rule or
statistic gives again over the inputs its provenance record names. It exists because fifteen of the
sixteen `exact` steps of this repository's own processes compute a value from their inputs alone —
a verdict, an order, a branch name — and none of the five rows fits them. UC-4.13 says such a
step is then not `exact`; DEC-0173 asks the owner whether it is.

### 2. A step declares its checks beside its class
`Step.json` gains `checks`: a list of at least one check. A `wait` or `human` step declares none,
as it carries no class (ADR-0018). A step of any other class may declare checks; only `exact`
must.

### 3. The refusal happens at registration
A bundle whose `exact` step declares no check does not register. The finding names the
catalogue, row by row, with what each covers, does not cover and needs, and says where a step goes
when none fits: to a person, or to `sourced` with the check that fits. That finding is the content
of the conversation UC-4.13 asks for when a person writes the bundle by hand. A recomputation
declared on a step that does not give the same result again — anything but `rule` or
`statistics` — is refused too.

The rule is held where a bundle becomes a version, not as an invariant of every `ProcessVersion`.
A version stored before this decision has no checks, and recovery reads stored versions. An
invariant would make a stored version unreadable, and a run in progress could not resume.

### 4. The statement is generated by a rule, and never stored
`process.domain.service.exactness.statement` derives the statement from the version's steps.
Every sentence about a check is its catalogue row's sentence, filled in with that check's own
parameters and nothing else. So no check is described as covering more than its row says. A
result-producing step without a check is named in every part as unchecked. The residual risk
names, per step, what slips through all of its checks at once, and ends with the measured rate:
not yet measured, until the value ledger counts results a person corrected after the checks
passed.

A statement lacking any of the four parts does not exist: its constructor refuses it. Nor does one
that says "guaranteed", which a subject written by a person could otherwise bring in. Registration
builds the statement, and a version whose statement cannot be built is refused.

The statement is computed whenever it is read, from the version itself. It is never written to a
file or a table, so it cannot drift from the version: a changed check is a changed statement.
`taktusctl exactness --process <bundle>` prints it, or the findings that refuse the bundle.

## Alternatives
- **The catalogue inside the process component.** UC-4.13 places it in the shared kernel. A
  worker, a view and a third-party tool must read the same vocabulary, and the shared kernel is
  where that vocabulary lives (ADR-0016).
- **One check object per row, as a `oneOf`.** It fixes the parameters as tightly. A flat object
  with a rule per row lets the Python binding stay one closed type, as every other shared type is.
- **`checks` required for `exact` in `Step.json`.** It would refuse the check of a step from a
  bundle the contract cannot see whole, and every stored step of the past. The registration is
  where a bundle is judged whole.
- **Store the statement with the version.** A stored text can drift from its version, and UC-6.9
  forbids editing it. Generated on reading, it cannot drift.
- **A free-text description per check.** It would let a check be described as covering more than
  its row. The sentences are the catalogue's; only the parameters vary.

## Consequences
- Every `exact` step of the example and blueprint bundles declares a check: `verify-answer` of
  the worked example declares bounds, the other fifteen a recomputation under DEC-0173.
- A statement can be read for every bundle, by a test and by a person, before anything runs.
- No migration: a step's checks are part of the stored step document, and a step stored without
  them reads as before.
- Running the checks is #223; the statement in the dashboard, a run and a report is #224.

## Where this promise ends
The checks are declared, not run: until #223 a declared check changes no result, and the
statement says what the version claims, not what any run did. The refusal holds for a bundle that
registers; a version stored before it, or one built in code without registering, is not refused.
The catalogue's sentences describe what a row can catch; they cannot tell whether the row was the
right choice for the business, which stays the user's (UC-4.13). A subject is a person's words and
is shown as written, except that it may not say "guaranteed". Recomputation stands only while
DEC-0173 is unanswered or answered for it. The measured rate is "not yet measured" until the value
ledger counts it.
