Some acts always stay with a person, however much of the work Taktus carries out on its own.
Signing, paying, or setting a direction are examples. Your organisation decides which acts these
are. When the work reaches such an act, Taktus stops before it and asks the person whose role it
is to decide.

## What you receive

A **decision request**. It says what is to be decided, and gives you the options. One of them is
recommended, with the reason. It also says by when the answer is needed. Today you find your
requests in Taktus's list of decisions; a request to the owner of the organisation also reaches
the owner in the chat.

## How you answer

You answer with the option you choose: the letter alone, such as "B", or "option B". Taktus reads
your answer by a fixed rule and never guesses. Words you add are kept with the decision, but
Taktus does not act on them.

Taktus then tells you how it read your answer. **Nothing happens until you confirm that
reading.** Only you can confirm it. An answer from which Taktus cannot read exactly one option
changes nothing, and Taktus asks you again.

## While you have not answered

The work waits at that step. Steps that do not depend on your decision go on. Nothing waits in
silence: the request names who it waits for and by when, and one that is late is marked as late.

How long you take to decide is yours to see. Other people see such times only added up per role
or department, never per person.
