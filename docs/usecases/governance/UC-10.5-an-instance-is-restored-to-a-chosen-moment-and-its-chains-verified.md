# UC-10.5 — An instance is restored to a chosen moment, and every chain verified

## 1. What must be achieved

An operator knows, before anything fails, how much work a failure can lose and how long the
instance stays down. Each install profile states both: the **recovery point objective**, the
longest stretch of recent work a loss of the database may take with it, and the **recovery time
objective**, the longest time from the decision to restore until the instance works again.

An operator who wants a smaller loss than one backup a day enables **point-in-time recovery**:
the database's write-ahead log is archived off the node as it is written, so that the instance can
be restored to any moment the archive covers, not only to the moment of the last backup. The
write-ahead log is the database's own record of every change, written before the change is
applied. Enabling it is the operator's choice. The smallest install works without it.

That the objectives hold is shown, not assumed. A **restore drill** restores a backup to a chosen
moment, away from the running instance, and checks the result the way an auditor would: every
tenant's ledger chain verifies, and it holds the entries a digest taken before the restore names.
A database that merely starts is not a restored instance. The restore stays a procedure a person
carries out with standard tools, without Taktus.

## 2. How it is verified

- An automated test restores a database to a moment between two batches of ledger entries, from
  a base backup and its archived write-ahead log. Every tenant's chain verifies afterwards. Each
  tenant's newest entry is the one a digest taken at that moment names. No entry of the later
  batch is present.
- The same test shows the drill failing where it must: an entry altered in the restored database
  is reported as a broken chain, and a moment the archive does not reach is reported as not
  restorable. Neither is reported as a success.
- The drill's chain check needs no Taktus code. It recomputes every hash by the ledger's published
  rule, and a test holds its verdict to that of Taktus's own verification on the same entries.
- The drill never writes into the archive it restores from, and never touches the running
  instance's database.
- Every install profile of the cluster deployment, and the single-server installation, states its
  recovery point objective and recovery time objective in its operating documentation. Each
  profile names how the drill measures the part of the recovery time that is the machine's.
- The restore to a chosen moment is written as steps of standard commands — an object store
  client, the encryption tool where backups are encrypted, and the database's own tools. Every step
  can be carried out by hand without Taktus.
- Point-in-time recovery is off unless the operator enables it, and the smallest profile installs
  and backs up without it.

## 3. Where the boundary lies

**Not continuous availability.** A standby database that takes over within seconds is the highly
available profile's external database and its operator's. This use case covers a restore, which
takes the instance down. **Not a promise the platform cannot keep.** The time a person needs to
notice a failure and decide to restore is stated as an objective and not measured. Only the
machine's part is measured. **Not the restore of the running instance by Taktus.** Taktus does
not put itself back into production (ADR-0013 D). The drill restores into a scratch database,
and only a person restores the instance itself. **Not every moment.** A moment before the oldest
kept base backup, or after the newest archived segment of the log, cannot be restored, and the
drill says so.

## 4. What it rests on

The business-critical requirements, a restart without data loss (ADR-0013 A) and repair without
Taktus (ADR-0013 C); the backup by a job of the chart and the restore by hand (ADR-0087); the
reporting of a failed backup (ADR-0106); the ledger's hash chain and its rule (ADR-0006,
ADR-0164); the remediation plan executable by hand (UC-4.12). The roadmap's `1.0.0` names
point-in-time recovery and recovery targets per install profile (issue #346), and *The road to
1.0.0* names the restore drill S-03 that verifies the ledger chain. Source:
`docs/research/2026-10-12-devops-review.md` §K2, DEC-0187.

## 5. What is proven so far

The restore to a moment between two batches of entries, by the drill and by the steps by hand,
ending at the heads noted at that moment and without the later batch
(`tests/deploy/test_point_in_time.py`); the drill run with no network at all, and its archive
unchanged afterwards; a moment beyond the archive and an altered entry failing it. The drill's
chain check held to Taktus's own on entries Taktus wrote — run chains, consumption, anchors — and
on every kind of alteration (`tests/deploy/test_drill_chain.py`). Point-in-time recovery off by
default, and the chart's archive, base backup and drill as rendered (`tests/governance/test_chart.py`).
The objectives per profile are stated in `deploy/k8s/README.md` §2 and the guide's page
*Restoring*; no test reads them. On one machine the backup program schedules itself, a backup
per period and a drill after the backup of every week, and a drill that fails is recorded for the
owner as a failed backup (ADR-0222, `tests/deploy/test_backup_schedule.py`); run once on a compose
project of its own, it wrote the backup and a verified drill report. Not yet run: a drill on an
installed instance.
