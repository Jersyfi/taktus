# UC-11.2 — Data-protection requests are answered from the ledger, and the evidence is generated

## 1. What must be achieved

A person whose data an organisation processes may ask what is held about them, have it
corrected, have it erased, or have it handed over in a form another system can read. These are
the data-protection tools: **access**, **rectification**, **erasure** and **portability**. A
request names its **subject**: the person or organisation the data is about, by the identifier
the organisation's own systems use.

Taktus answers such a request from what it already records — the ledger, the provenance chain,
the runs, their artifacts and the traces inside their steps — without anyone writing a query by
hand. Taktus prepares the answer. A person checks who asked, confirms, and carries it out. An
erasure removes what Taktus holds of the subject and leaves the record of what happened intact
and verifiable. Every request and its outcome are themselves recorded.

The organisation can also show, at any time, what Taktus processes for it, where, by which
adapter, and how long it keeps it. That evidence is generated from the installation's
configuration and the ledger. Nobody writes it by hand, so it cannot drift from what runs.

## 2. How it is verified

- A request names a kind — access, rectification, erasure or portability — and a subject. Taktus
  lists every run, step, artifact, stored result and trace that holds data of that subject, each
  by its identifier, and prepares the export or the erasure. No query is written for it. A test
  runs cases for two subjects and finds every holding of the one asked for and none of the
  other's.
- A prepared request changes nothing. A request is carried out only when a person confirms it,
  and the confirmation states that the person checked the requester's identity. A confirmation
  without that statement, or of a request already carried out or withdrawn, is refused and
  changes nothing.
- An erasure removes the content Taktus holds of the subject: the runs' inputs, their stored
  results and artifacts, and the traces of their steps. Afterwards the ledger verifies from its
  first entry, and the provenance chain of every run the erasure touched verifies. A test checks
  both after an erasure, and finds none of the erased content readable.
- Content that another run, not of the subject, still references is not removed by the erasure.
  The prepared erasure names it and says why it is kept.
- An export — for access and for portability — is one machine-readable document of the subject's
  holdings with their content, generated when a person confirms the request. An access export
  also states the processing behind each holding: the adapter, where it processes, and how long
  each kind of record is kept.
- A rectification lists what Taktus holds of the subject and every result that was built on it
  or left the system. Taktus never rewrites the record of a run that happened. Correcting a
  value at its source, or a result that left the system, stays with a person (ADR-0022).
- Every request is recorded in the ledger when it is prepared, when it is carried out and when
  it is withdrawn, with who acted. The ledger entry names the request, never the subject or any
  of its data (ADR-0006).
- The **evidence** of an installation is one document, generated on request from configuration
  and the ledger: every process step that handles personal data, the adapter that serves it, the
  location the adapter declares, the tenant's residency rule, which capabilities serve personal
  data, how long each kind of record is kept, how much each adapter was used over a recent
  period, and how many requests were prepared, carried out and withdrawn. A test changes the
  configuration and finds the evidence changed with it.

## 3. Where the boundary lies

**Not legal advice.** Taktus does not decide whether a request is lawful, which exemption
applies, or what a deadline is. The evidence is not a records-of-processing template for any
particular business; it is marked as not legally reviewed. **Not identifying the requester.**
Checking that the person asking is the subject stays with a person. **Not the source systems.**
Taktus erases what it holds itself. The data in the organisation's own systems is erased or
corrected there, by a person or a process of its own. **Not data that was never declared.** A
subject is found where a step served the subject's case (UC-7.7); data of a subject carried by a
run without a case scope is not found. **Not backups.** A backup taken before an erasure holds
the erased content until the backup itself is gone. **Not the identifiers in the record.** The
ledger and the provenance chain are written once and never changed; the provenance record of a
case names its subject's identifier and keeps it (ADR-0021, ADR-0130). **Not watching a
deadline.** A process that serves data-protection requests declares its own deadline (UC-4.17).

## 4. What it rests on

Definition `UC-11.2`, the data-protection tools, kept with the definition's meaning
(`NUMBERING.md`). The content-free ledger (ADR-0006); the provenance chain (ADR-0021); the case
scope that names a subject (ADR-0130, UC-7.7); the trace inside a step and its retention
(ADR-0135); the object store (ADR-0165); the register of what acts for a tenant (ADR-0147,
UC-7.8); the residency rule (ADR-0116, UC-11.1); a correction after the fact stays with a person
(ADR-0022). UC-03 B-3.08: the request recognised, the export or erasure prepared, carrying it
out and confirming it kept with a person. The milestone `1.0.0` of `docs/roadmap.md`.

## 5. What is proven so far

ADR-0176 builds it. The named tests prove every condition of §2 against the memory stores,
with real runs of a scoped process for two customers: the holdings of one customer found
through the provenance chain and the intake, and none of the other's; a run whose scoped step
has not completed found through its intake; preparing changes nothing; a confirmation refused
without the identity check, after a withdrawal, a second time, and when the holdings changed
since preparation; an erasure that removes the inputs, stored results, artifacts and trace,
keeps the object another run references, leaves a run in flight alone, and after which the
ledger verifies from its first entry and the run's provenance chain verifies; an access export
with the processing beside each step and the keeping of each record, a portability export with
the data alone, neither stored; a rectification that lists what left the system and rewrites
nothing; every request's entries naming the request and who acted, never the subject; the
evidence changed by a change of the tenant's declaration, residency rule, trace retention and
the ledger. The object store's removal is proven for every adapter, the S3-compatible one
among them; the provenance store's lookup by subject and the trace's erasure per run against
PostgreSQL too (`tests/adapters/persistence/`).

Not built: a REST route and a page in the web app, and a blueprint process that prepares a
request as one of its steps (UC-03 B-3.08).
