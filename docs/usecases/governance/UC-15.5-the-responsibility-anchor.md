# UC-15.5 — The responsibility anchor — every domain has a person

## 1. What must be achieved

Full autonomy without responsibility would be a bus factor of zero of another kind. Every domain an
organisation instantiates — a department run by Taktus — therefore has exactly one human owner: the
process owner, the management, or a named specialist. The owner sees the domain in their view,
receives its escalations, decides its autonomy raises, and is the addressee of its takeover test.

Acts that are legally binding are catalogued. The **legal-anchor catalogue** lists, per domain and
per jurisdiction, the acts that always stay with a person — filing a tax return, a signature, a
termination, releasing a payment above a threshold, a data-protection notification, concluding a
contract. It can be configured, and it can never be reduced to nothing.

## 2. How it is verified

- A domain without an owner cannot be instantiated, and a domain whose owner leaves is shown as
  without owner until a new one is named. While it has none, nothing in it is raised in autonomy.
- Every escalation of the domain (UC-4.5), every proposal to raise one of its processes' autonomy
  (ADR-0026), and its takeover test (UC-6.3) are addressed to the owner.
- A legal anchor holds whatever the autonomy level of the process: an act it names halts the run at
  the step boundary and raises a decision request (UC-7.4), at level 4 as at level 1.
- The catalogue is configurable per domain and per jurisdiction. A configuration that would leave a
  domain with no legal anchor at all is refused.
- The catalogue in force appears in the generated conformity evidence of the organisation
  (definition `UC-11.3`).
- For every domain it is shown whether its takeover test and its removal test have passed; a domain
  where either has not is not shown as able to run without Taktus.

## 3. Where the boundary lies

**Not legal advice.** The catalogue is a list Taktus enforces, not a legal assessment; which acts
belong in it for a jurisdiction is reviewed by people with that expertise, and who reviews it is
DEC-0029. **Not the person's performance.** The owner is the addressee of the domain's duties, never
the subject of a metric about how they discharge them (principle 14). **Not the bus-factor index
itself.** The index, computed from the takeover and removal tests, is definition `UC-6.7`, migrated
in step 4; this use case requires only that each domain shows whether both passed. **Not
strategic or correction anchors.** Those are the tenant's set too (ADR-0008, ADR-0022), and this use
case requires only the legal class per domain.

## 4. What it rests on

Anchors and their rule that the set is reducible and never empty (ADR-0008); the correction anchor
beside it (ADR-0022); the autonomy statement and the proposal of a raise (ADR-0026); the takeover
test (ADR-0013 B, UC-6.3); `docs/vision/personas.md`, which states the responsibility anchor as one
of two roles carrying an obligation; `docs/architecture/governance.md` §2. The legal-anchor list
there is the project's own and not legally reviewed for any jurisdiction. A domain is instantiated
from a blueprint (definition `UC-15.1`, migrated in step 3), which arrives with the catalogue in
`0.6.0`, this use case's version. Definition `UC-15.5`.
