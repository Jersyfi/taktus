# UC-10.2 — Administration

## 1. What must be achieved

Taktus has a level of administration for running and managing it: users, roles, groups and tenants, the
mapping of channel accounts to identities, the management of connectors, workers and models with their
maturity, the allocation of resources and hardware, updates, backups, and the health of the system.

Taktus itself supports most of the administration — "create tenant X with the standard roles" — and
every administrative act can always be done the classic way as well, through a user interface or a
command line. Every administrative act is audited.

## 2. How it is verified

- Every administrative act is available through the command line and through the web app. A test takes
  the list of administrative acts and finds each in both.
- An administrative act asked of Taktus in dialogue is carried out as a plan that a person with the right
  commissions (UC-1.2). It needs the same right as the same act done by hand (UC-7.3), and never a wider
  one.
- Every administrative act, by whatever path, is a ledger entry naming the identity that did it, the act
  and its target.
- The mapping of a channel account to an identity can be seen and revoked by an administrator (UC-1.7).
- An adapter's maturity is shown where it is managed, as the catalogue records it, and cannot be set by
  hand (UC-8.9).
- A backup can be listed and its age seen by an administrator; restoring one asks a person first
  (`CLAUDE.md` §9: a restore counts as potentially destructive).
- An update of Taktus prepared in dialogue is applied only when a person commissions it, and the version
  before it can be restored without Taktus (ADR-0013 C). For the Taktus project's own instance, a version
  Taktus built is deployed by another instance or by a person, never by itself (ADR-0013 D).

## 3. Where the boundary lies

**Not the organisation's own identity source.** Where the organisation has single sign-on, identities come
from there; Taktus maps them and does not replace it. **Not the infrastructure.** Administering the
platform an instance runs on is not this use case and not that instance's (ADR-0025). **Not rights.** What
a role may do is UC-7.3; administration is where it is granted.

## 4. What it rests on

Every command belongs to one identity (UC-1.7); the organisation's structure (UC-1.4); rights and least
privilege (UC-7.3); maturity and the removal test (UC-8.9); planning in dialogue (UC-1.2); the
business-critical requirements (ADR-0013), among them that an earlier version can be restored without
Taktus;
where an instance may run (ADR-0025). Definition `UC-10.2`, whose identity mapping UC-1.7 already carries.
Filed in `identity`, which owns tenants, accounts, roles and channel identity (NTC-0064). The roadmap names
no version; `0.3.0`, with the web app, is the session's proposal.

**What an accepted decision supersedes in the definition's text.** The definition lists the allocation of
resources and hardware among what Taktus administers with its own support. No instance administers the
infrastructure it runs on (ADR-0025), so for its own platform the allocation is among Taktus's own work —
which work may use which resources — and never a change to the platform. For the Taktus project alone,
the definition's updates are narrowed as well: a version Taktus built is put into production by another
instance or by a person (ADR-0013 D).

## 5. What is proven so far

Built by ADR-0279, ADR-0295 and ADR-0296. The inventory of administrative acts
(`adapters/driving/inventory.py`) names each act with its command, its operation and its page in
the web app, and the first named test finds each in both ways in: identities, keys, roles,
rights, channel links, quotas per unit, the structure, projects, tenants with their export and
import, adapters with their maturity (shown, never set by hand), the backup's listing, the
instance's health, and an update of Taktus. Every act that changes something is a ledger entry
naming the identity that did it. The mapping of a channel account is seen and revoked by an
administrator (UC-1.7).

An administrative act asked in dialogue is a plan draft carrying the act; commissioning it needs
`tenant.administer`, the right the same act needs on the HTTP surface, is refused with nothing
written without it, and records `plan.commissioned`, `administration.carried_out` and the act's
own entry, each naming the commissioner. The acts a plan carries out and the acts that stay by
hand, each with why, are together the inventory, which a test holds. The backup lists its newest
runs with their age on the command line and in the web app; neither offers a restore, which the
guide *Restoring* carries out without Taktus. An update of Taktus is prepared as a plan of a
person's steps — backup, upgrade, check, and the way back to the release before — and
commissioning it records `instance.update_commissioned` and applies nothing (ADR-0013 D).

Not proven by these tests, and why the state is `built`: that the release before an update is
restored without Taktus is the guide *Upgrading*, section 5, which a person exercises on an
installed instance, as the restore drill does for a backup (ADR-0222). A read — a list, the
health, the backup's listing — is answered and is not a ledger entry.
