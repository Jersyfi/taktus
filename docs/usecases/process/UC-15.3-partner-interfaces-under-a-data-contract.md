# UC-15.3 — Partner interfaces under a data contract

## 1. What must be achieved

An organisation exchanges data with its partners — suppliers, customers, service providers,
authorities. Taktus connects them through open exchange formats where they exist: electronic
invoices, classic electronic data interchange, documented interfaces, files in open formats, and
portals without an interface through a connector. It handles both directions: on import it
receives, validates, maps into the organisation's own systems and acknowledges; on export it
produces, checks, sends and confirms. The mapping between a partner's format and the
organisation's own data is built by Taktus from sample data in dialogue with a person, and kept
versioned.

Every partner interface has a documented **data contract**: the fields, which are mandatory, the
frequency, and who is responsible on each side. A deviation from it is detected and escalated,
never silently corrected.

## 2. How it is verified

- No data is exchanged with a partner without a data contract. A process version that imports from
  or exports to a partner without one does not register.
- The data contract is part of the process version: changing it is a new version, and the mapping
  is versioned with it.
- Every received file or message is checked against the contract before anything of it is mapped:
  a missing mandatory field, a changed shape or an unexpected frequency marks the import as
  deviating, with the field and the clause it breaks (UC-4.10, source shape). A deviating import is
  escalated and is never corrected, completed or guessed by Taktus.
- Every export is checked against the contract before it is sent, and a failing export is not
  sent.
- Where an open standard exists for an exchange, the process uses it; a proprietary format is used
  only where the partner offers nothing else, and the process version says so.
- Partner data is held to the tenant's data-residency rules and to the access rights of the
  systems it came from (UC-11.1).
- Every send is recorded as data that has left the system (ADR-0022).

## 3. Where the boundary lies

**Not federation.** Where the partner is itself a Taktus instance, this use case prepares an exchange
between instances and does not require one (`docs/vision/beyond-1.0.md`). **Not the partnership.**
Negotiating a data contract and settling a dispute stay with people. **Not an integration
platform.** Taktus drives the exchange through connectors and the organisation's own systems; it is
not a product for electronic data interchange (principle 1). **Not every format.** Which formats can be read depends on the
connectors that exist; the requirement is that any format with a connector can be held to a
contract.

## 4. What it rests on

The process version and its bundle (ADR-0011); the source-shape checks of UC-4.10 and the
result-defect terms of ADR-0021, because a partner's changed export is one of the cases ADR-0021
names for a result defect; the connector contract with its effects (ADR-0024) and egress entries (ADR-0022);
data residency (UC-11.1); escalation (UC-4.5). Definition `UC-15.3`. The roadmap names no milestone
for partner interfaces; `0.7.0`, where the first domain after engineering arrives, is proposed in
DEC-0069.
