# UC-15.4 — End-to-end chains across domains

## 1. What must be achieved

Most of the value of an organisation run by Taktus lies between its departments: order to cash —
offer, order, delivery, invoice, payment, booking; procure to pay — need, order, receipt of goods,
invoice check, payment; the administrative part of hire to retire; incident to resolution. Taktus
links the processes of the domains into chains that work as closed loops (UC-4.6). At every
boundary between domains it hands over the context and the documents completely, and it shows the
chain as a whole (UC-4.2).

## 2. How it is verified

- A chain is a declared object of its own: which processes it links, in which order, and what
  passes at each boundary between domains.
- At every boundary the handover is recorded: which data and which documents went across, and why
  — the clause of the chain that required them. A test runs a chain of two domains and finds, at the
  boundary, every item the receiving process read listed in the record of the handover, and nothing
  the receiving process read that is not.
- A handover that would leave out an item the chain declares does not happen: the chain halts at
  that boundary and escalates (UC-4.5).
- A chain has service-level objectives and a value balance of its own, beside those of its
  processes.
- An anchor in one link of the chain stops the whole chain at that link's step boundary — not
  before it, not after it (ADR-0005, ADR-0008); the links before it keep their results, and the
  chain resumes at that boundary once the person has decided.
- Every link keeps its own control mode: a link controlled from an external tool and a link
  controlled by Taktus can stand in one chain (UC-5.2).

## 3. Where the boundary lies

**Not a merger of the processes.** Each link stays a process with its own version, owner and
autonomy level; the chain links them. **Not the value balance's figures.** How value is measured is
the value ledger's (definition `UC-9.3`); a chain has its own balance, computed there. **Not a
process across tenants.** A chain runs inside one tenant; across organisations it is UC-15.3's
partner exchange. **Not the domains themselves.** Which domains exist is the blueprints' (definition
`UC-15.1`).

## 4. What it rests on

Step boundaries and the stop at them (ADR-0005); anchors (ADR-0008) and the responsibility anchor
(UC-15.5); the activity log, which records every handover (UC-6.1); the provenance chain across
runs and processes (ADR-0021), which a handover extends; the diagram (UC-4.2); control inside or
outside per process (UC-5.2). Definition `UC-15.4`. The roadmap names no milestone for chains
across domains; `0.7.0`, where a second domain exists to chain to, is set by DEC-0069.

## 5. What is proven so far

A chain is a declared object: the process versions it links, in order, and at each boundary every
item handed over — the input it fills, the step it comes from, its clause — held to the versions
it links (ADR-0274). The instance stores it, starts it, and shows it as a whole on the HTTP
surface and in the web app: its links, what passes at every boundary with its clause, its
objectives and its runs (ADR-0301). Only the role `owner` declares, starts and decides a chain
(NTC-0277), and a reader sees a chain only where they may see every process it links.

A chain of two domains runs link by link: the handover is recorded with every item and its
clause, and the receiving process is given exactly those items and nothing else. A handover that
would leave out a declared item does not happen: the chain halts at that boundary, and the
situation package — what happened, what was tried, what is affected, the options with their
risks, what is needed — reaches the role the chain names in the owner-facing channel (ADR-0302).
The person gives the lacking items, and the chain goes on by itself with them, each marked as
given by a person; or the person stops it.

An anchor in one link stops the whole chain at that link's step boundary: the step before it
finished, the anchored act did not happen, and no later link started. Once the person decided,
the chain goes on by itself from that boundary, and the links before keep their results; the
same holds for a link that waits for a person's answer. A chain has objectives of its own — the
time one run of the whole chain may take and the share of its runs that finish — and is measured
against them once it ends; its value balance is the sum of its links' balances in the value
ledger (ADR-0288). A link controlled by an external tool stands beside one controlled by Taktus
in one chain: only the decoupled link asks the tool, and a task the tool withdraws stops that
link while the coupled one before keeps its result — all by the named tests.

Where the promises end: a chain has no trigger of its own beyond a person or a caller of the
HTTP surface; it goes on at the scheduler's next look, within a minute; and a halted chain whose
report stays unanswered is not recorded as overdue (ADR-0301, ADR-0302).
