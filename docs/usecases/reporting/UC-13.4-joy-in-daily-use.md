# UC-13.4 — Joy in daily use

## 1. What must be achieved

Taktus should be a pleasure to use, and used every day. A person sees their small successes — "3 h
40 min regained this week" — has a personal dashboard of their own automations, and has a first
experience that succeeds in minutes, not hours.

Success is told as time regained and as the person's own goals reached. It is never told as points
without substance.

## 2. How it is verified

- A person's dashboard shows their own automations, what each did in a period, and the time it
  regained. The time regained is computed from the runs and the time the same work takes by hand, as
  the value balance states it (UC-9.3); it is never a figure set for effect.
- The dashboard is the person's own. It is visible to them alone by default, and showing it to anyone
  else is their act, which they can withdraw (as UC-13.5).
- Success is shown as time and as goals the person set and reached. There are no points, badges,
  levels, streaks, rankings or comparisons with other people.
- A person new to Taktus completes a first automation that runs in under 30 minutes, measured in the
  trial of UC-10.3.
- The successes are shown where the person already is — the web app, or as a short line in their
  channel at the interval they choose, or never (principle 9).

## 3. Where the boundary lies

**Not gamification.** Nothing in this use case rewards use for its own sake. **Not a view of the
person for anyone else**: what Taktus took off a person, as others may see it, is UC-13.5, at process
level. **Not onboarding itself**, which is UC-10.3.

## 4. What it rests on

The value balance and its time by hand (UC-9.3); a person's own contribution made visible (UC-13.5);
usability for people who are not technical and its trial (UC-10.3); views (UC-6.4). Filed in
`reporting` as a view, epic E13 (ADR-0029). Definition `UC-13.4`. The roadmap names no version; `0.5.0`,
with the value ledger the time regained is computed from, is the session's proposal.
