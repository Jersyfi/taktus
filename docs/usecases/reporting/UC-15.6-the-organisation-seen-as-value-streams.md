# UC-15.6 — The organisation is seen as value streams, not as an org chart

## 1. What must be achieved

A tenant's processes are shown as value streams: from the customer's need to the customer's
outcome, between the management processes above them and the support processes below. Each
stream shows its autonomy and method mix, its flow efficiency, its blocked share and the
department boundaries it crosses. A stream opens as swimlanes, one for each role that acts along
it. A value stream is an object of its own: it has a kind, a trigger, an outcome and one
responsible role, and it extends the chain of UC-15.4.

## 2. How it is verified

- A value stream is declared with its kind — core, management or support — its trigger, its
  outcome, one responsible role and its processes in order. A changed declaration is a new
  revision, and the one before is kept. A declaration naming a process the tenant does not have
  is refused, naming it.
- The map shows every stream in its tier, and lists the processes no stream names.
- Each stream shows the share of its steps at each autonomy level and of each method kind, its
  flow efficiency and blocked share over the runs of its processes, the cause that held up most,
  and each department boundary it crosses, named by the steps on either side.
- A stream opens as swimlanes: a column per process in the stream's order, a lane for Taktus's
  reproducible methods, one for its variable methods, and one per role a person acts in. Each
  step is in its column and its lane.
- The view reads only existing records and stores nothing of its own (ADR-0029). A test reads
  the map and the swimlanes and finds the stores as they were.
- The organisation's structure, each identity's `org_path`, stays as it is, and is overlaid on
  the lanes: each role lane names the units its holders sit in.
- No figure names a person. A role's waiting is shown only where at least two identities hold
  the role (ADR-0015). Another tenant sees no stream and no process of this one.

## 3. Where the boundary lies

**Not drawing processes as the way to build them** (a non-goal): the view shows processes, it
does not edit them. **Not the chain's handovers.** What passes at each boundary between processes,
and the stop when an item is missing, is UC-15.4's. **Not a role profile.** What a role must be
able to take over, as a position description, is not required here. **Not a simulation.** What a
change of level would shift between Taktus and the role lanes is UC-9.1's. **Not a second
responsibility anchor.** Whether a raise goes to the stream's role or the domain's owner is
UC-15.5's.

## 4. What it rests on

The chain of processes across domains (UC-15.4), which this object is the declaration of; a
person's step names a role (UC-4.20, ADR-0146); reporting reads and owns no figure (ADR-0029);
the withholding of a role's figure (ADR-0015); how a process really runs (UC-9.7); the blocked
time accounts (ADR-0043). The owner accepted it as F1 of the product outlook (DEC-0190), and
issue #338 carries it; it waited for the role at every person's step (#331). ADR-0159 §4 to §6 is
how it is built. The version is `0.6.0`, the milestone DEC-0190 places it in.

## 5. What is proven so far

A stream is declared at `PUT /streams/{stream}` by the role `owner`, kept as revisions, and
refused where a process is missing. `GET /streams` draws the three tiers with each stream's
mix, flow, blocked share and crossings; `GET /streams/{stream}` its swimlanes with the units
overlaid; the web app's pages are `#/streams` and `#/streams/{stream}`. Reading either leaves the
stores as they were. A role held by one identity shows no waiting. Another tenant sees none of
it — by the named tests. Until UC-15.4's chain runs, a stream's figures are those of its
processes' own runs (ADR-0159, *Where this promise ends*).
